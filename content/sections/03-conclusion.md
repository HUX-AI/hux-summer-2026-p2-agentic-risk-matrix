---
title: Conclusion and next steps
status: published
---

Agentic AI moves the governance question from what a model says to what an agent is permitted to do. Present frameworks name that risk without saying what control it requires. The contribution here is the assessment method: a taxonomy of observable failure modes, an autonomy scale a use case can be assigned to reproducibly, a scenario library that tests the method against real deployments, and a matrix that states a posture at every intersection and the evidence owed at every level.

Three extensions are proposed: a crosswalk to ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A control identifiers, multi-agent coverage for the inter-agent and cascading-failure risks the taxonomy names but no scenario exercises, and a live engagement with use cases the team did not write. A fourth follows from the treatment of supervision — system transparency should become an assessment dimension in its own right, because an approval step cannot function as a control where the reviewer cannot see the reasoning, the tool use or the data behind a decision.
